Level 1 · Research Narrative

Research Journey: Engineering, Optimization & Computational Intelligence

Trained initially in Electronic Engineering (Instrumentation and Control) and later completing an M.S. in Industrial Engineering at Instituto Tecnológico de Querétaro (ITQ), my interest in mathematical modeling grew directly out of practical manufacturing questions. Graduate research on production-line optimization using genetic algorithms showed how evolutionary search could navigate constrained, non-linear engineering trade-offs where manual tuning and linear approximations fall short.

Over the following decade and a half, that computational toolkit expanded through sustained, peer-reviewed collaboration with academic research groups in Mexico — including Universidad de Guanajuato, CIATEQ, Universidad Autónoma de Querétaro, Instituto Tecnológico de Aguascalientes, and Instituto Tecnológico de Celaya — conducted in parallel with an industrial engineering and automation career through Innovación Integral de Sistemas (2002–2009) and Exxerpro Solutions (2010 onward).

Across chemical and process engineering applications — from dividing-wall distillation and thermally coupled sequences to renewable biojet fuel production — the methodological core remained consistent: couple rigorous process simulators directly to constrained multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, map full Pareto trade-off fronts, and introduce artificial neural networks as surrogate models when full physical simulation becomes computationally prohibitive.

That historical research in evolutionary computation and surrogate modeling is distinct from my more recent work in generative AI, large language models, and retrieval-augmented architectures (RAG). Yet the engineering discipline connecting them is the same: model the domain faithfully, evaluate competing constraints quantitatively, and validate every computational output against real operational requirements.

Level 2 · Research Contributions

Four Connected Lines of Work

Themes A through C represent documented, peer-reviewed research published between 2008 and 2019; Theme D describes how those modeling and optimization principles carry forward into current industrial AI development.

THEME A Peer-Reviewed Research

Multi-Objective Optimization

Constrained genetic algorithms, Pareto-front exploration, and optimization under competing engineering objectives — balancing energy demand, total annualized cost, equipment complexity, and dynamic controllability.

THEME B Peer-Reviewed Research

Computational Modeling & Neural Surrogates

Integration of rigorous process simulation (Aspen Plus, MATLAB), evolutionary algorithms, and neural-network surrogate models to accelerate convergence on computationally expensive, non-convex engineering problems.

THEME C Peer-Reviewed Research

Process Intensification & Sustainable Engineering

Dividing-wall columns, extractive and reactive thermally coupled distillation, energy integration, and sustainable aviation fuel (biojet) and biodiesel production routes validated for both efficiency and operability.

THEME D Current Engineering Practice

Applied Computational Intelligence

Methodological continuity from mathematical optimization and surrogate modeling into contemporary industrial AI — including governed LLM workflows, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), knowledge graphs, and advisory diagnostic software.
